UNESCO Heritage Sites Analysis

Analyzed UNESCO World Heritage Site data in Tableau to compare site classifications, endangered status, geographic distribution, and land coverage across countries.

Tableau • Filters • Data Visualization

Project Overview

This project used UNESCO World Heritage Site data to explore how protected heritage sites are distributed and classified around the world. I created Tableau visualizations that made it possible to compare different types of sites and examine differences between countries.

Site Classification Analysis

I compared UNESCO sites based on their classification as cultural, natural, or mixed heritage sites. Separating the sites into these categories made it easier to understand the different types of locations represented within the World Heritage dataset.

Endangered Sites

The analysis also examined sites identified as endangered. This added another dimension to the dataset by distinguishing sites facing greater preservation concerns from the broader collection of World Heritage Sites.

Country-Level Analysis

I analyzed the data geographically to compare countries based on the number of World Heritage Sites they contained. Tableau made it possible to visualize the geographic distribution of these sites and compare countries more efficiently.

Land Coverage

In addition to counting the number of sites, I examined the physical area dedicated to World Heritage Sites. This provided another way to compare countries because a country with many sites does not necessarily dedicate the greatest amount of land to heritage protection.

Visualization Approach

Tableau was used to create visual comparisons across the UNESCO dataset. Filters allowed different classifications and groups of sites to be explored, while geographic visualizations helped communicate country-level differences.

Combining site counts with physical land coverage also allowed the dataset to be examined from two perspectives: how many protected sites a country contained and how much physical area those sites represented.

Project Takeaway

The project demonstrated that World Heritage Sites can be compared in several different ways. Site classification and endangered status describe characteristics of the sites themselves, while country-level counts and land coverage provide geographic measures for comparing heritage protection across locations.
