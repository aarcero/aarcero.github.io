Warby Parker A/B Test

Evaluated an A/B experiment using statistical hypothesis testing to determine whether the difference between the control and test groups was statistically significant.

Excel • A/B Testing • Hypothesis Testing

Project Overview

This project used A/B testing and statistical analysis to compare the performance of a control group and a test group. The goal was to determine whether the difference observed between the two groups represented meaningful evidence of a change or could reasonably have occurred because of random variation.

Analysis

I compared the mean values of the control and test groups and performed a statistical hypothesis test in Excel. The control group produced a mean of 3.3373, while the test group produced a slightly lower mean of 3.2767.

Although the two sample means were different, comparing the averages alone was not enough to determine whether the experiment produced meaningful evidence of a difference. The hypothesis test was therefore used to evaluate the statistical significance of the observed result.

Hypothesis Test

The statistical test produced a p-value of 0.1494. Using a significance level of 0.05, the p-value was greater than the threshold required to reject the null hypothesis.

Key Findings

Statistical Conclusion

The experiment did not provide sufficient statistical evidence to conclude that the control and test groups performed differently. Although the control group had a slightly higher sample mean, the p-value of 0.1494 was greater than the 0.05 significance level.

Therefore, the null hypothesis was not rejected. The observed difference between the two groups could reasonably be explained by sampling variation rather than the experimental change.

Recommendation

The experimental change should not be treated as producing a statistically demonstrated improvement based on these results. The difference between the groups was not statistically significant, so there was not enough evidence from this test alone to support changing the existing approach based on performance.

Additional testing could provide more evidence before making a larger decision. A future experiment could collect additional observations and continue evaluating whether a consistent difference develops between the groups.
