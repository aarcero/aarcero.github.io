Grammy Capstone Project

Analyzed website performance data to uncover user behavior, identify trends, and develop data-driven recommendations.

Excel • Data Analysis • Data Visualization • Pivot Tables • Formulas

Project Overview

This project analyzed website performance for Grammy.com and RecordingAcademy.com to evaluate user engagement and determine whether separating the two websites improved the user experience. Using Excel, I analyzed website traffic, pages per session, bounce rate, average time on site, mobile usage, and A/B testing results to identify changes in visitor behavior and develop a business recommendation.

Key Findings

The Recording Academy website showed the strongest overall engagement, with 2.78 pages per session, a 33.7% bounce rate, and an average time on site of 128.50 seconds. After the website split, Grammy.com increased from 1.86 to 2.25 pages per session and its bounce rate improved from 41.6% to 40.2%, although average time on site decreased from 102.85 to 82.99 seconds.

Mobile visitors represented 73.7% of total website traffic, making mobile usability an important factor in the overall user experience. Traffic analysis also showed major increases surrounding Grammy-related events, including approximately 1,389,590 visitors on a Grammy Awards live-event day compared with roughly 32,388 visitors on a typical day.

A separate A/B test found that the test group produced 158 CTA clicks from 4,099 visitors compared with 119 clicks from 3,797 visitors in the control group. The test version achieved approximately a 3.85% conversion rate compared with 3.13% for the control, and the difference was statistically significant.

Recommendation

Based on the analysis, I recommended maintaining the separate Grammy and Recording Academy websites while continuing to improve the mobile experience. The website split showed improvements in important engagement metrics while allowing each site to maintain a more focused purpose for its audience.

I also recommended increasing awareness of Recording Academy programs and services throughout the year rather than relying primarily on Grammy-related events. One approach would be to highlight Recording Academy initiatives alongside Grammy content on the homepage, allowing the Grammy Awards to attract visitors while exposing those users to additional Recording Academy content.
