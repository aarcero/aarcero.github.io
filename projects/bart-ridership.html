BART Ridership Analysis

Analyzed BART ridership data to compare weekday and weekend travel patterns, evaluate Yellow Line usage, and determine how much of the system's ridership came from downtown stations.

Excel • PivotTables • Data Analysis

Project Overview

This project analyzed Bay Area Rapid Transit ridership records to better understand how passengers use the transit system. The analysis focused on differences between weekday and weekend travel, Yellow Line ridership, and the percentage of total ridership associated with downtown stations.

Data Preparation

I used Excel to organize and prepare the ridership data for analysis. The project included creating additional variables from the original data using functions such as MONTH, WEEKDAY, LEFT, and IF. These variables made it possible to classify records and compare different types of ridership activity.

Analysis

I compared weekday and weekend ridership to determine how passenger demand changed depending on the day of the week. The analysis showed stronger weekday activity, indicating the importance of commuter travel within the BART system.

I also analyzed Yellow Line usage to understand the amount of ridership associated with one of BART's major routes. This analysis identified high-volume Yellow Line usage as an important part of overall passenger activity.

Finally, I evaluated downtown ridership to determine whether most passengers were concentrated in downtown stations or distributed throughout the larger BART network.

Key Findings

Weekday Travel

Ridership was higher during weekdays than weekends, demonstrating the importance of weekday commuter demand within the BART system.

Yellow Line

The analysis identified high-volume Yellow Line usage, making the route an important area to consider when evaluating passenger demand.

6,932,181

Downtown Ridership

Downtown stations accounted for more than 6.9 million passengers in the analyzed ridership data.

28.2%

Share of Total Ridership

Downtown stations represented approximately 28.2% of total ridership, meaning roughly 71.8% of ridership occurred outside the downtown stations.

Recommendation

The ridership patterns show that service planning should account for strong weekday commuter demand and high-volume routes such as the Yellow Line. These areas represent important opportunities for service optimization and infrastructure planning.

The downtown analysis also shows that most ridership occurred outside downtown stations. Because downtown stations represented only 28.2% of total ridership, planning decisions should consider passenger demand across the broader BART network rather than concentrating resources only on downtown locations.

Skills Applied

Excel, PivotTables, data preparation, Excel formulas, ridership analysis, comparative analysis, and translating transportation data into operational recommendations.
